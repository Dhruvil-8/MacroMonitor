# Learning Note: Real Effective Exchange Rates (REER) and Global Liquidity

## What is a Real Effective Exchange Rate?
A nominal exchange rate (e.g. USD/INR) measures the relative price of two currencies. A **Real Effective Exchange Rate (REER)** measures a currency's purchasing power against a trade-weighted basket of foreign currencies, adjusted for domestic and foreign inflation differentials:

$$REER = \prod_{j=1}^k \left( \frac{P \cdot E_j}{P_j^*} \right)^{w_j}$$

Where $P$ is domestic price index, $P_j^*$ is foreign trading partner price index, $E_j$ is nominal exchange rate, and $w_j$ is the trade weight.

### Valuation Interpretation:
- **REER > 100 or High Percentile (>75th)**: Currency is structurally strong or overvalued in purchasing power terms, making exports less competitive and imports cheaper.
- **REER < 100 or Low Percentile (<25th)**: Currency is structurally undervalued (e.g., Japanese Yen following multi-year monetary divergence).

## Global Reserve Adequacy and FPI Flows
For emerging markets such as India, central bank foreign exchange reserve accumulation serves as a buffer against volatile Foreign Portfolio Investor (FPI) outflows. Reserve adequacy is assessed relative to **import cover** (minimum 6-8 months benchmark recommended by IMF; India maintains >11 months).
